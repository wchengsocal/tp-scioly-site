What it asks of you

Three or four events

Each with one or two partners. Weekly meetings for every event you're in, plus study on your own and with your partner between them.

  • Attendance is tracked at event meetings
  • Build events need extra construction and testing time
  • Competition days are a full day, start to finish
What you get back

Costs are reimbursed

Relevant, reasonable purchases are fully reimbursed when you turn receipts in to the coordinators — build materials shouldn't be why you skip an event.

  • Medals for top-20 placements in your events
  • Team trophies on overall performance
  • Topics you can't get in a normal class schedule

Questions people actually ask.

What if I don't get the event I wanted?

It happens, and it's usually fine. An event you didn't ask for is a chance to try something you'd never have picked — a lot of people find what they're actually good at that way.

How much time does this take?

Weekly meetings for each of your events, plus studying on your own and with your partner, plus any extra work your coaches assign. Build events take the most — construction and testing add up. The one real failure mode is procrastinating.

Who's on my team?

The club fields multiple teams of fifteen. Your team is you, your partners, and their partners — so you'll know the people you compete beside.

How does a competition day work?

You're there the full day and compete in your events as they're scheduled. Medals go to top-20 placements, and team trophies are awarded on overall performance.

Do I have to pay for my own materials?

No. Relevant, reasonable purchases are fully reimbursed — keep the receipts and turn them in to the coordinators.

Do I need experience to apply?

No. The club is open to anyone interested in science and STEM, and events are assigned partly on what you say you want to learn.
