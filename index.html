Events

Twenty-two events this season, divided into study, lab, and build. Most members compete in three or four of them, with one or two partners on each. Here's what falls into each group.

Written tests, and for many of them you bring a binder you've been building since the fall. You study on your own and with your partner between meetings, so the work spreads across the whole season instead of piling up the week before a tournament.

  • Anatomy and Physiology
  • Astronomy
  • Botany
  • Designer Genes
  • Disease Detectives
  • Dynamic Planet
  • Remote Sensing
  • Rocks and Minerals
  • Codebusters

Hands-on work at a bench, under a time limit, with a partner. Graders look at your method as well as your final answer, which is why the weekly practice matters.

  • Chemistry Lab
  • Circuit Lab
  • Engineering CAD
  • Experimental Design
  • Protein Modeling
  • Thermodynamics
  • Water Quality

You design something in the fall and you'll probably still be revising it in the spring. Builds take the most time outside meetings. Keep your receipts and the club pays you back for materials.

  • Boomilever
  • Electric Vehicle
  • Hovercraft
  • Mission Possible
  • Ping-Pong Parachute
  • Wright Stuff

Season Calendar

Three invitationals, then the San Diego regional. Teams that place well there go to the state tournament in April, alongside the top teams from every other Southern California regional.

  1. Sep 9

    Information meeting

    Start here. We explain what the club does, how the events work, and what the year will ask of you, all before you commit to anything.

  2. Sep 23

    Application deadline

    Your application tells us which events you want. We weigh that against your schedule, which events run at the same time on competition day, and any experience you already have.

  3. Sep 27

    Teams released

    We post the event assignments, partners, and team rosters. Weekly meetings start that week, and so does construction for anyone on a build event.

  4. January

    USC Invitational

    The largest in-person Science Olympiad tournament in the country, with both Division B and Division C competing. It runs in January, and we'll post the exact date once registration opens.

  5. February

    Golden Gate Invitational

    Students from Stanford and Berkeley run this one, and it's the largest invitational on the West Coast. Long trip north, and the strongest field you'll see before regionals.

  6. Date TBD

    UCSD Triton Invitational

    UCSD's third year hosting this one, and it's the closest tournament to home. Registration hasn't opened yet, so there's no date to give you.

  7. February

    San Diego Regionals

    Every event runs on one day, and you get a single attempt at it. The top twenty in an event medal, and the highest-placing teams move on to state.

  8. April

    Southern California State

    The top teams from all nine Southern California regionals, and the last competition of the season.

How to Join

There's no entry exam. Come to the information meeting on September 9, get your application in by the 23rd, and you'll have your events and your team by the 27th. We build each schedule around the events people asked for.

What it asks of you

Three or four events, one or two partners on each, and a weekly meeting for every event you're in. The rest is what you and your partner put in between those meetings.

  • We take attendance at event meetings
  • Builds need extra time for construction and testing
  • Competition days last from morning to evening

What you get back

Give your receipts to a coordinator and you get the money back, as long as the purchase was reasonable and went toward your event. Cost shouldn't be the reason anyone skips something.

  • A medal for placing in the top twenty in your event
  • Team trophies based on how everyone on the team places
  • Subjects you won't get in a normal class schedule
